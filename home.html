Texas Towing Complaint Guide

Who to contact, what to say, and the deadlines that matter if your car was towed or booted from private property in Texas.

Safety comes first. Your life is worth more than your car. Never get inside, or stand under or beside, a vehicle that is being hooked up or lifted. If a tow operator lifts or moves a vehicle with a person inside, or anyone is hurt, call 911. You can sort out the paperwork afterward.

Do these first

  1. Get safe and get help if needed. If anyone was hurt or put in danger, call 911 and ask for a report number.
  2. Write down the facts now while they are fresh: date, exact time, address, what you saw, what the driver said, and the truck’s company name, plate and number.
  3. Take photos and video of the truck, the tow operator’s license number if visible, the parking signs (or lack of signs), and your car’s condition.
  4. Mark your calendar. A tow hearing request has a short deadline (see below). Missing it is the most common way people lose their options.
  5. File your complaints using the list below. You can do several at once.

Your rights at the moment of the tow

Car is not fully hooked up

You can ask for it to be released without paying anything, and you do not have to prove ownership.

Car is fully hooked up but has not left the lot

You can keep your car by paying a drop fee. You do not have to prove ownership. The driver must accept cash, credit and debit cards. The statewide maximum is $135 for most cars (see fee table).

Car already left the property

Call the number on the tow-away sign to find it. You can get it back from the vehicle storage facility (VSF) after showing ID, proving you are the owner or authorized user, and paying the allowed fees. At the VSF you can access the car for free to get ownership papers or personal belongings.

Source: TDLR “Consumer Information about Towing.” Cities and counties can set lower limits than the state maximums, so check your local rules too.

If a person was inside the vehicle when it was lifted or moved

  1. Call 911 if there is any injury or ongoing danger. Otherwise call the local police non-emergency line and file a report. Ask for the report number.
  2. Get medical attention if you feel any pain, even if it seems minor, and keep the records.
  3. File a TDLR complaint and describe exactly what happened, in order, with times.
  4. Ask the property manager in writing to preserve security camera footage, and ask the towing company in writing to preserve dashcam video, dispatch records and GPS logs (template below).
  5. Consider talking to a lawyer. Texas Occupations Code Chapter 2308 includes civil-liability (§ 2308.404) and criminal-penalty (§ 2308.405) sections, and whether they apply depends on the facts and the current text of the law. A lawyer or the court can tell you.

Where to file a complaint

These are separate tracks. Doing one does not replace the others.

Deadline: file fast

1. Justice of the Peace court: tow hearing

Use this if you want a judge to decide whether the tow was justified, and to order a refund if it was not. File with any Justice of the Peace court in the county where the tow happened. The request must be made before the 14th day (not counting weekends and national holidays) after your car was placed in the storage facility or booted. The court may charge a $20 filing fee, and the hearing must be held within 21 days of the court receiving your request.

Your request must include: your name, address and phone; where and when the car was towed; who authorized the tow; the name and contact info of the tow company and the storage facility; a copy of any receipt or notice; and photos of the parking signs, or a statement that no sign was posted. The tow company and storage facility must give you the information you need to fill this in.

No deadline stated

2. Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR)

TDLR licenses tow companies, tow operators and storage facilities and can investigate and penalize them. It does not get your money back for you, which is what the tow hearing is for. Complaints about a tow company, an operator, a storage facility, a booting company, or their employees all go here.

  • Online: tdlr.texas.gov/complaints
  • Phone: (800) 803-9202 (in Texas) or (512) 463-6599
  • Mail: TDLR, Tow Trucks and Vehicle Storage Facilities, P.O. Box 12157, Austin, TX 78711
  • You can check any tow operator or company’s license at TDLR license search.

3. Police

Call 911 for danger or injury. For a report after the fact (threats, an unsafe lift, damage, theft of belongings), use the non-emergency line of the police department or sheriff for where it happened. Ask for the report number and a copy.

4. Property manager or owner

The property owner authorized the tow and usually has a contract with the tow company. Email the manager (keep a copy) describing what happened, ask for the parking rules and sign information in writing, and ask them to preserve camera footage. Many problems get resolved here.

5. Local city or county office

Some cities set lower fee caps or run their own towing units (Dallas, for example, takes towing reports through 311). Search “[your city] towing complaint” for local options.

6. Texas Attorney General and legal help

The Attorney General’s Consumer Protection Division takes consumer complaints at texasattorneygeneral.gov. For legal help, try TexasLawHelp.org or a local legal aid office. If you were injured or your car was damaged, contact your insurer and consider a consultation with a lawyer.

You must get an itemized tow ticket showing the company’s licensed name, phone, registration number and the operator’s TDLR license number. Storage facilities cannot add other fees beyond those allowed.

Paying to get your car out does not end your options. You must pay allowed fees to retrieve it from storage, but you can say you are paying under protest, keep every receipt, and still file for a hearing and a complaint.

Evidence checklist

  • Photos and video of the truck, the lot, every parking sign (close-up of the text), and any damage
  • Tow ticket and every receipt, with itemized charges
  • Tow company name, phone, certificate number, and operator’s TDLR license number
  • Name and address of the storage facility, and the property’s management company
  • Your lease parking rules and any registration or permit records for your vehicle
  • A written timeline made the same day, with exact times
  • Names and phone numbers of witnesses
  • Police report number, medical records, repair estimates if relevant
  • Copies of every email or text with the property manager or tow company

Apartment cameras and tow-truck dashcams are often overwritten within days, so ask for preservation right away.

Copy-and-paste templates

Fill in the brackets. Stick to facts. Calm, specific complaints get taken more seriously.

A. Complaint description for TDLR or the tow hearing

On [date] at about [time], my [year, make, model, color, plate] was towed or hooked up at [property name and address] by [tow company], operator [name, license number if known].

What happened, in order:
1. [Where the car was parked and whether signs were posted]
2. [What you saw when you arrived or what the driver did]
3. [Anything said by the driver or staff]
4. [Fees charged, and whether you were offered a drop fee]

I believe this tow or the operator’s conduct did not follow Texas towing rules because: [for example: no compliant sign, car was not fully hooked up and I was not allowed to take it back, fees above the allowed maximum, no itemized ticket, unsafe handling].

Attached: [photos, tow ticket, receipts, report number].
I am requesting an investigation and, where applicable, a refund of fees.

B. Letter to tow company and property manager (with preservation request)

Subject: Request for review and evidence preservation: tow incident on [date] at [property]

Hello,

I am writing about a tow incident on [date] at about [time] at [property name and address] involving my [vehicle description, plate].

[Two to four factual sentences describing what happened, in order.]

I am asking for the following:
1. Preserve all records related to this incident, including driver dashcam video, dispatch and call logs, GPS and equipment logs, tow tickets, and any security camera footage covering [location] between [time range].
2. Provide an itemized copy of all charges and the company’s nonconsent tow fee schedule.
3. Tell me whether you will refund [charges] voluntarily.

I intend to use the tow hearing process and file a complaint with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation if this is not resolved. Please reply by [date, about 5 business days out].

Thank you,
[Name]
[Phone] | [Email] | [Plate]

A letter to the company does not stop the tow hearing deadline. File the hearing request first if time is short.

This is general information, not legal advice. Towing rules, fee limits and agency procedures change, and local rules can differ. Always confirm details with the official sources below, and talk to a lawyer about your specific situation.
